How to make a logo with a transparent background (PNG) free

Updated: July 2026 · Reading time: 5 minutes

You have the logo, but it came with a white background: on an invoice it leaves a box, on a colored website you see the patch, and over a photo it's simply unusable. What you need is the logo with a transparent background, a PNG with only the symbol and the text, nothing behind, so it sits on any color or image. This guide shows how to get that for free, with a couple of details that separate a clean cutout from one with a "halo".

When you'll need it

  • Web and social: the logo on a colored header or over a photo, without the white box.
  • Documents and invoices: letterheads, PDFs and templates where the logo background shouldn't cover anything.
  • Presentations: slides with a dark background where a white-background logo would stick out.
  • Merch and email signatures: anywhere the logo has to blend in, not be pasted on.

Step by step

  1. Open the logo image in PhotoCut Studio, at the highest resolution you have.
  2. Use the AI Cutout or the box to separate the logo from the white background; touch up the fine outline details with the brushes (letter edges, thin lines, inner counters).
  3. In Export, choose Transparent mode.
  4. In the preview, check that the logo looks good on both a light and a dark background, with no white halos.
  5. Download as PNG, at a large size so you can shrink it later without losing sharpness.

We exported as a transparent PNG: that way the logo looks good on any background (a website, an invoice, a t-shirt). All in the browser, without uploading your brand.

The detail that ruins logos: the white halo

The most common mistake when removing a logo's background is leaving a halo: a thin rim of near-white pixels around the outline. On a light background it's invisible, but the moment you put the logo on a dark color a ghost edge appears. The way to catch it is simple: check the logo on a dark background in the preview before exporting. There any white leftover jumps out, and you can clean it with the brushes.

Counters: the holes that also have to be transparent

A logo with an "O", an "A" or a symbol with an inner hole needs that hole to also be transparent, not white. If the cutout only removes the outer background, the inner counters stay filled with white and the logo looks "blocked" on colored backgrounds. Go over those holes with the erase brush so they let the background through.

Transparent PNG vs. vector logo (SVG)

It helps to set expectations. Removing the background produces a PNG, which is a pixel map: it looks sharp at the size you exported, but if you enlarge it a lot it pixelates. For a logo that has to scale to any size —from a favicon to a billboard— the ideal is a vector SVG, made in a vector editor, which never loses quality. That said, for 90% of uses (web, documents, social, presentations) a large transparent PNG works perfectly. If you need several fixed sizes (for favicons or app icons, say), you can generate them from the cut-out logo.

Why PNG and not JPG

Same as with signatures: JPG doesn't keep transparency. A logo in JPG always brings a color background, so the moment you put it on another color the rectangle shows. PNG keeps the transparent background and the logo blends in anywhere. WebP also supports transparency if your target allows it, but PNG is the most compatible.

Without uploading the logo anywhere

If the logo is a client's or not yet public, you don't want it floating around other people's servers. In PhotoCut Studio the cutout happens in your browser: the image isn't uploaded anywhere or stored when you close the tab.

Frequently asked questions

Does the result work as a vector logo (SVG)?

No. Removing the background produces a transparent PNG, which is a pixel map: sharp at the exported size but it pixelates if you enlarge it a lot. To scale to any size without losing quality you need an SVG made in a vector editor. The PNG covers most web and document uses.

How do I avoid the white halo around the logo?

Touch up the outline with the brushes and check the result on a dark background in the preview: on dark, any leftover white shows immediately and you can fix it before exporting.

Why does the logo have to be PNG and not JPG?

Because JPG doesn't keep transparency and fills the background with a color. A logo in JPG drags a background rectangle that shows over any other color. PNG keeps the transparency and the logo blends in anywhere.

Is the logo uploaded to a server?

No. The cutout is done in your browser: the image isn't uploaded anywhere or stored when you close the tab — useful when the logo is a client's or not yet public.
