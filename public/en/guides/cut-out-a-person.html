How to cut a person out of a photo without visible edges

Updated: July 2026 · Reading time: 6 minutes · By Gabriel Maglia

Cutting a person out of a photo —to drop them on another background, make a collage, a flyer or simply isolate them— has an easy part and a hard part. The easy part is the general silhouette: automatic cutout solves that in one click. The hard part is the edges: hair, fingers, the gap between arm and body. That's where you can tell whether a cutout is well done or looks like a sticker. This guide is exactly about that: how to go from the automatic cutout to a result that doesn't scream "cut out".

The workflow that works: automatic first, brushes after

The temptation is to do it all by hand or expect the automatic to be perfect. Neither. The efficient workflow is in two layers:

  • Layer 1 — AI Cutout: detects the person and does 90% of the work in a second. It leaves the silhouette resolved.
  • Layer 2 — Brushes: you only fix the hard 10%. A keep brush to recover what got eaten and an erase brush to remove leftover background.

Thinking of it this way saves a lot of time: you don't go over the whole silhouette, just the trouble spots.

Step by step

  1. Open the photo you want to cut the person from in PhotoCut Studio.
  2. Apply the AI Cutout: it detects the figure and separates it from the background.
  3. With the keep and erase brushes, fix the hard areas: strands of hair, fingers, the space between arm and body.
  4. Choose the background: transparent, a color, or a new image.
  5. Export as PNG or WebP if you need transparency; as JPG if the final background is solid.

We tested it: where the edge was weak

We cut out a half-body portrait (1201 × 1500 px) on a deliberately hard background: dark colors very similar to the subject's clothing. The AI Cutout resolved the whole silhouette well up front, but —as often happens when background and clothing share a tone— a faint fringe stayed on the shoulders. Not on the hair, which came out clean: on the jacket's outline.

It was fixed in under a minute with the remove brush, lowering the brush size to trace the edge precisely. The practical lesson: automatic gets you to 95%; the minute of brushing is what keeps it from looking like a sticker. All of this ran in the browser, without uploading the photo anywhere.

The hair: the acid test

If a person cutout looks bad, it's almost always the hair. A hair outline cut "in a block" —like a helmet— is the unmistakable mark of a rushed cutout. To keep it natural:

  • Work with zoom. Get close to the hair area; at full size you won't see the fine strands.
  • Small brush on the edges. A big brush wipes out detail. On the hair outline, shrink it.
  • Don't fight impossible loose strands. Some isolated hairs against a complex background can't be saved; a tidy outline beats one with weird fringes.
  • Check against the final background. Hair that looks fine on gray can show halos on a dark background. Try it on the real background before exporting.

Several people, or one from a group

Automatic cutout detects the people in the scene. If you want to keep all of them, you touch up each one's edges. If you want to isolate just one from a group, it helps to use the box first to narrow that person's area and then refine with the brushes, painting to erase the others.

What this cutout doesn't do

Worth clarifying so you don't get frustrated. Cutting out a person is for isolating them (keeping them, moving them to another background, placing them in a design). What it does not do is fill the gap a deleted person leaves in the middle of a scene —to "remove someone from the photo" and have the background complete itself you need a content-aware erase, which is a different feature. Here the focus is a clean cutout of the figure.

Photos with people don't leave your device

Photos with people tend to be the most personal. In PhotoCut Studio the cutout happens in your browser: they aren't uploaded to any server or stored once you close the tab.

Frequently asked questions

Can I cut out several people from the same photo?

Yes. Automatic cutout detects the people in the scene; with the brushes you refine to keep them all, or paint to erase whoever you don't want to include. To isolate just one from a group, the box selection helps narrow the area before touching up the edges.

How do I keep the hair from looking cut out in a block?

Let the automatic cutout resolve the silhouette and then touch up the strands with a small brush and zoom, keeping what is hair and erasing the background that sneaks between the strands.

Does it work to remove a person who's in the way?

Partially. You can isolate the person you want to keep and discard the rest. What it doesn't do is fill the gap a deleted person leaves in the middle of a scene: that needs a content-aware erase, which is a different feature.

Is the photo with people uploaded to a server?

No. The whole cutout happens in your browser. Photos with people aren't uploaded to any server or stored once you close the tab.
