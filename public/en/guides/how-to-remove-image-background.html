How to remove the background from an image free (and without uploading it)

Updated: July 2026 · Reading time: 5 minutes · By Gabriel Maglia

Removing the background from a photo is probably the most repeated edit in the world: for an online store, a WhatsApp sticker, a profile photo or an app icon. Most popular tools solve it by uploading your image to a server, processing it there and sending back the result — which means your photo travels, is stored who-knows-how-long and is usually limited by a paywall. There's another way: process it right in your browser, without the file leaving your device.

The three methods (and when to use each)

1. AI segmentation: the fast path

A neural network trained to separate "subject" and "background" analyzes the image and generates a mask automatically. It's the best starting point for photos with people, pets or products on reasonably distinguishable backgrounds. In PhotoCut Studio this is the "AI Cutout" button (shortcut I): the model downloads once, runs in your browser with WebAssembly and works even offline after the first visit.

2. Interactive cutout: you mark, the algorithm decides

When the AI gets confused — translucent products, very busy backgrounds, several subjects — the classic GrabCut algorithm is still unbeatable: you draw a rectangle around the subject and the algorithm estimates which pixels belong to the foreground by analyzing color and contrast. It's deterministic and predictable: if the result isn't right, you adjust the rectangle and you're done.

3. Refine brushes: fine control

No automatic method is perfect at hard edges. That's why the right workflow always ends with two brushes: keep (marks areas the cutout removed by mistake) and remove (erases leftover background). With zoom on the canvas you can work at pixel level.

The recommended workflow, step by step

  1. Open the photo (or paste it with Ctrl/Cmd+V from the clipboard).
  2. Try the AI Cutout first. On most photos it's already good.
  3. If it fails, draw a box tight around the subject.
  4. Turn on the before/after compare to spot leftovers.
  5. Refine the edges with the keep/remove brushes (zoom with the wheel).
  6. Adjust the edge feather (1–3 px is usually natural).
  7. Export as a transparent PNG — or apply a color background, sticker or shadow.

We tested it: and confirmed the photo never left the browser

We cut out a real image (1201 × 1500 px) with the AI Cutout and, meanwhile, kept the browser's network tab open. We confirmed what the local method promises: there was no upload of the image to a server — the u2netp model (4.6 MB) downloads once and from then on all the compute happens on your device.

As for the result: the automatic left the silhouette ready in one go, and we only had to run the remove brush over a fringe on the edge where the background had a tone similar to the clothing. The "after" image above is that export, with the background 100% transparent.

Tricks for hair and fine edges

  • Resolution rules: cut out on the largest image you have and downscale later; the other way around loses detail.
  • Moderate feather: 1–3 px of feather makes the edge natural; more than 5 px looks "cut with blunt scissors".
  • Small brush on the hair: several passes with an 8–12 px brush beat one with a 40 px brush.
  • Check on light and dark backgrounds: light halos only show on a dark background, and vice versa. Use the white/black preview before exporting.

Which format to export?

FormatWhen
PNGThe standard with transparency: stickers, logos, icons, print.
WebPTransparency with much smaller files — ideal for the web.
JPGNo transparency: only if you apply a color or image background.

Common mistakes when removing a background

  • Confusing "transparent" with "white". If you export as JPG (which doesn't support transparency) the gap is filled with solid white or black. To keep transparency you need PNG or WebP.
  • Not checking at real zoom. At preview size, background leftovers and halos don't show; zoom to 100–200% before calling the cutout done.
  • Cutting out on an already-reduced photo. If the image comes compressed or low-res, the algorithm has less contrast information to decide the edge. Always start from the largest version available.
  • Overusing the "remove" brush. Running it too close to the subject eats parts that do belong to the figure (fingers, strands of hair). Several soft passes beat one aggressive one.

Why does it matter that the image isn't uploaded?

ID documents, photos of minors, unreleased products, client material under NDA… a good part of what gets cut out daily is sensitive information. If the tool processes the image in your browser, that question disappears: the file never leaves your device, there's no server to hack or retention policy to read. That's the underlying technical difference between PhotoCut Studio and cloud cutout services.

Frequently asked questions

Which format should I export to keep transparency?

PNG or WebP. Both keep the alpha channel; WebP produces much smaller files for the web. JPG does not support transparency, so use it only if you apply a color or image background.

What if the AI cutout gets the edge wrong?

Fall back to the interactive box (GrabCut): draw a rectangle around the subject and the algorithm decides the foreground by color and contrast. Then refine with the keep/remove brushes at zoom.

Is my image uploaded to a server?

No. The whole cutout runs in your browser with WebAssembly and a local AI model. The file never leaves your device — there's no server to hack or retention policy to read.

Does it work on hair and fine detail?

Yes, if you start from a high-resolution image and refine the hair with a small brush and zoom. A moderate edge feather of 1–3 px keeps the outline natural.
