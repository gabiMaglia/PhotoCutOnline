How to remove the location (GPS) and metadata from a photo

Updated: July 2026 · Reading time: 5 minutes · By Gabriel Maglia

Every photo you take with your phone stores, inside the file, data you don't see: the device model, the date and —most sensitive— the exact GPS location where you took it. That's the EXIF metadata, and it travels with the photo when you share it. This guide explains why it matters and how to remove the location before publishing or sending an image, without uploading it anywhere.

Why it matters (more than it seems)

A seemingly innocent photo —your pet, something you're selling, your car— can carry your home address inside if you took it there. If you post it on a classifieds site, send it to a stranger, or upload it to a site that doesn't strip metadata, you're sharing your location without realizing. For anyone selling online, taking orders or simply caring about privacy, this is a real risk that's easy to avoid.

What data a photo stores

  • GPS location: latitude and longitude of where it was taken (the most sensitive data).
  • Device: make and model of the camera or phone.
  • Date and time: the exact moment of capture.
  • Settings: shot parameters and, sometimes, the software it was edited with.

Step by step to remove it

  1. Open the photo in the metadata viewer.
  2. Check what it carries: it tells you if it includes GPS location.
  3. Tap "Download without metadata" to get a clean copy.
  4. Share that copy, not the original.

Don't the networks strip it automatically?

Sometimes yes, sometimes no. Big networks usually remove metadata on publish, but not all of them and not always — and if you send the original photo by email, by uncompressed messaging, or upload it to any site, the location travels with it. Relying on "the network will strip it" is not a strategy; removing it yourself before sharing is.

You don't lose quality

Removing metadata doesn't degrade the image: the photo is regenerated without the hidden data, keeping the visual quality. The photo looks the same; it just stops carrying your location, the device model and the date.

Do it without uploading your photo

It would be absurd to upload your photo to someone else's server to protect your privacy. In PhotoCut everything happens in your browser: reading the metadata and the clean copy are done locally, and the image isn't uploaded anywhere.

Frequently asked questions

Do all photos store the location?

Many do: phones usually save GPS by default. You can see what a photo carries by opening it in a metadata viewer.

Do social networks strip the location?

Sometimes, but not all and not always. If you send the original through other channels, the location travels with it. Better to remove it yourself first.

How do I remove the location without losing quality?

Regenerating the image discards the metadata while keeping the quality. The photo looks the same; it just loses the hidden data.

Is it safe to do this online?

In PhotoCut, yes: everything happens in your browser, the photo isn't uploaded anywhere.
