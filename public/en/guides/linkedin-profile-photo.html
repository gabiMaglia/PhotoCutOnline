LinkedIn profile photo: size and a professional background, free

Updated: July 2026 · Reading time: 6 minutes · By Gabriel Maglia

The LinkedIn profile photo is, for many people, the first professional impression they make online — a recruiter sees it before reading a single line of your experience. You don't need a photo shoot: a decent front-facing photo and a tidy background are enough for a polished image. This guide covers the right size, how to get a professional background from a casual photo, and the details of the circular crop that almost no one takes into account.

The right size

  • Format: square (1:1). LinkedIn accepts other aspect ratios and lets you frame it afterwards; the simplest is to upload it already square.
  • Size: between 400×400 px and 7680×4320 px. Within that range, 800×800 px is a safe bet that looks sharp everywhere (that figure is my usual value, no official source).
  • Maximum file size: 8 MB, so you won't fall short exporting in good quality.
  • File format: JPG or PNG (GIF isn't accepted). JPG is perfect if the background is solid; PNG for maximum sharpness.

Official source: LinkedIn Help, “Photo won't upload to your profile”, verified on 2026-10-06.

The circular-crop detail

This is the point that ruins many profile photos: you upload a square image, but LinkedIn shows it cropped into a circle. That means the four corners of the square are lost. If you push the face against an edge, part of the face or hair falls outside the circle. The practical rule: leave air around the head, with the face slightly above center, so the circle frames you nicely from the shoulders to the crown.

Step by step

  1. Choose a front-facing photo, from the shoulders up, with good light and a friendly expression.
  2. In PhotoCut Studio, use the AI Cutout to separate yourself from the original background; touch up the hair with the brushes.
  3. Frame it square with the face slightly above center and air above the head. If you use a square preset in Export, pick it in this step.
  4. Then add a professional background: a flat neutral color (Color mode) or a sober image (Image mode), lowering the opacity if needed. The background goes after the preset: choosing a preset sets the background back to white and you'd have to set your color again.
  5. Export square and large (for example 800×800) as JPG or PNG.

What background says "professional"?

Pure white works, but it can end up too "ID photo". For LinkedIn these usually look better:

  • Light gray or medium gray: neutral, modern, flatters almost any outfit.
  • Muted blue or a soft corporate tone: conveys seriousness without being boring; useful if your industry has an associated color.
  • A real, blurred and sober background: with Image mode you can add an office or neutral-wall background and lower its opacity so it doesn't compete with your face.

The rule is simple: the background is there to make you stand out, not to draw attention by itself.

Mistakes that cost points

  • A cropped group photo. Someone else's arm or a forced crop shows. Use a photo where you're alone.
  • Face against the edge. The circular crop will eat part of the head.
  • A chaotic original background poorly cut out. Leftovers of the old background around the hair look careless. Check the outline with zoom.
  • A very old or very informal photo. Consistency with how you look today matters more than aesthetics.
  • Uploading small. A 200 px photo looks blurry when the profile is enlarged.

Your photo doesn't leave your device

The cutout and background change happen entirely in your browser. Your photo isn't uploaded to any server or stored once you close the tab: you upload it, already finished, straight to LinkedIn.

We tested it: how much of the figure is lost when LinkedIn shows the circle

Image: quitar-fondo-antes.jpg, 720 × 900 px, 78.4 KB (public-domain portrait).

Tool: Cutout → AI cutout, square presets, Export → Color #c9ced6.

What I did:

  1. I loaded the portrait and applied “AI cutout”.
  2. I chose three square presets one by one: Amazon 2000×2000, Shopify 2048×2048 and Instagram post 1080×1080 (there is no LinkedIn preset), and noted which background each one left selected.
  3. With each preset I downloaded a transparent PNG to measure what percentage of the figure falls outside the circle inscribed in the square, which is what LinkedIn doesn't show.
  4. Then I set “Color” #c9ced6 (a sober gray), downloaded as PNG and as JPEG, and read the color of the four corners.

What I measured:

  • AI cutout: 4.1 s.
  • Amazon 2000×2000 (5% margin): 2000 × 2000 px, 196 KB JPEG; 0.1% of the figure falls outside the circle; corners #c9ced6 in PNG and #c9cdd6 in JPEG.
  • Shopify 2048×2048 (6% margin): 2048 × 2048 px, 198 KB JPEG; 0.0% of the figure falls outside the circle; corners #c9ced6 in PNG and #c9cdd6 in JPEG.
  • Instagram post 1080×1080 (8% margin): 1080 × 1080 px, 77 KB JPEG; 0.0% of the figure falls outside the circle; corners #c9ced6 in PNG and #c9cdd6 in JPEG.

What went wrong or what limit I saw: There is no LinkedIn preset. Each preset, when chosen, sets the background back to #ffffff and I had to set the gray again; with the gray set, the PNG comes out with exactly #c9ced6 and the JPEG shifts it slightly, to #c9cdd6, because of compression. I measured the whole figure against the circle, not just the face, and I didn't compare with the real crop LinkedIn does.

Tested by Gabriel Maglia on 2026-10-10 with PhotoCut (web) in Chrome 154.0.8037.99 · macOS 14.8.8 · PhotoCut version: commit e3d95d0.
