PNG, JPG or WebP: which to choose (and when)

Updated: July 2026 · Reading time: 5 minutes · By Gabriel Maglia

Choosing an image format sounds technical, but it comes down to two questions: do I need transparency? and is it a photo or a graphic?. With those, you solve almost every case. Let's go step by step.

What each one is, in a line

  • JPG (or JPEG): the classic format for photos. It compresses with loss (drops some detail to weigh less) and doesn't keep transparency.
  • PNG: made for graphics and transparency. It compresses losslessly (keeps all the detail) and does keep transparency, but it's heavy for photos.
  • WebP: the modern one. It can behave like JPG (lossy) or like PNG (with transparency), and generally weighs less than both at the same quality.

The question that decides everything: do you need transparency?

This is the point that causes the most confusion. If you cut out the background of an image —a logo, a signature, a product, a subject with no background— and you want that background to stay empty (to place it on any color), you need a format with transparency: PNG or WebP. If you export it as JPG, the transparent background is filled with a color (almost always white), and that box will ruin your cutout the moment you put it on another background.

Short rule: transparent background → PNG (or WebP). Solid background or normal photo → JPG or WebP.

The second question: is it a photo or a graphic?

  • Photo (people, landscapes, products): has millions of colors and soft gradients. There, JPG and WebP shine: they weigh little and the loss is barely noticeable. PNG, on the other hand, leaves it huge.
  • Graphic (logos, text, screenshots, flat illustrations): has sharp edges and few colors. There, PNG (or WebP) keeps the edges clean; JPG adds a blurry "noise" around the text.

Size: who wins

For the same photo, the usual size order is PNG (heaviest) > JPG > WebP (lightest). WebP usually achieves the same look as a JPG while taking up considerably less, which makes it ideal for fast websites. The downside of WebP is compatibility with old software; of PNG for photos, the weight; of JPG, that it has no transparency and degrades if you recompress it many times.

Cheat sheet by use case

  • Logo or signature with no background: PNG (or WebP). Never JPG.
  • Product photo on white for a marketplace: good-quality JPG (and optionally a transparent PNG as backup).
  • Photo for social media: JPG; PNG only if it has text or graphics with edges.
  • Image for your own website: WebP so it loads fast.
  • Sticker (WhatsApp, Telegram): the format the platform asks for (WhatsApp uses WebP), always with transparency.
  • Screenshot with text: PNG, so the letters stay sharp.

A detail: recompressing degrades (in JPG)

Every time you edit and re-save a JPG, it loses a bit more quality, because it compresses again with loss. If you're going to edit an image in several steps, work in PNG or WebP during the process and leave JPG for the final export. With PNG (lossless) you can save a thousand times without degrading.

In PhotoCut you can export in all three

When downloading your cutout you can choose PNG, WebP or JPG. The only combination that makes no sense —and that the tool prevents— is JPG with a transparent background, because JPG can't keep transparency. The whole process happens in your browser: your images aren't uploaded to any server.

Frequently asked questions

Does WebP display well everywhere?

In all modern browsers and most web platforms, yes. Where it can fail is old desktop software. For a modern website, WebP is a great choice; if in doubt, JPG or PNG are safe.

We tested it: the same photo as PNG, JPG and WebP, and what happens to transparency

Image: quitar-fondo-antes.jpg, 720 × 900 px, 78.4 KB (public-domain portrait).

Tool: File → PNG / JPG / WebP with quality.

What I did:

  1. I opened the photo in the “File” tab (original JPG) and converted it to PNG, JPG quality 80, WebP quality 80, JPG 60 and WebP 60, downloading each one.
  2. I repeated with logo-despues.png (820 × 637 px, 32 KB, PNG with a transparent background) as PNG, JPG 80 and WebP 80.
  3. I measured the real weight of each download and, for the logo ones, the percentage of transparent pixels.

What I measured:

  • 78 KB photo: PNG 956 KB · JPG 80 80 KB · WebP 80 45 KB · JPG 60 60 KB · WebP 60 31 KB.
  • The File screen estimated “78 KB → 80 KB” for JPG 80 and the real download weighed 79.9 KB.
  • Logo with transparency (32 KB source): PNG 46 KB with 87.3% transparent; WebP 80 11 KB with 87.3% transparent; JPG 80 17 KB with 0.0% transparent (the corner turned rgb(255, 255, 255)).

What went wrong or what limit I saw: The photo's PNG weighed 12 times the original JPG: PNG isn't “better quality” for photos, just lossless. When I turned the logo into a JPG the transparency was lost (the corner turned white). I only measured weights: JPG 80 and WebP 80 aren't equivalent in visual quality and I didn't compare them by eye.
