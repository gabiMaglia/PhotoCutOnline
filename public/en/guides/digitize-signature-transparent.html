How to digitize a signature with a transparent background (PNG) free

Updated: July 2026 · Reading time: 5 minutes · By Gabriel Maglia

You need to sign a PDF, a contract or a form, and all you have is your signature on paper. The solution almost everyone tries —photograph it and paste it— fails on one detail: the photo brings the white paper background, so when you insert it over the document a white box appears covering the text. A well-made digitized signature goes with a transparent background: just the stroke, no paper. This guide shows how to get it in a couple of minutes, without uploading your signature to any server.

The starting photo is almost everything

A clean signature starts with a good photo or scan. Before cutting anything, take care of this:

  • Dark marker on plain white paper. A strong black or blue stroke on white gives maximum contrast, which is what makes separating the signature from the background easy.
  • Even light, no shadows. A shadow on the paper becomes a hard-to-remove gray smudge. Diffuse light, camera straight on.
  • Straight framing. Take the photo as perpendicular to the paper as possible so the signature isn't tilted or distorted.
  • High resolution. The larger the photo, the sharper the stroke when you shrink it later.

Step by step

  1. Sign with a dark marker on plain white paper and take a straight-on photo, with good light and no shadows.
  2. Open the signature photo in PhotoCut Studio.
  3. Use the box to keep only the signature area, and the Magic wand or the brushes to remove the white paper around the stroke.
  4. In Export, choose Transparent mode: only the signature remains, on an empty background.
  5. Download as PNG, the only format that keeps transparency, ready to insert into your document.

Why PNG and not JPG

It's the number-one mistake when digitizing signatures. JPG doesn't keep transparency: it always fills the background with a color, almost always white. If you insert a JPG signature over a contract, it drags that white rectangle and covers whatever is below. PNG keeps the transparent background, so only the stroke shows over the document. If your editor supports it, WebP also works, but PNG is the most compatible with Word, Google Docs and PDF readers.

How to insert it into your document

  • Word / Google Docs: Insert → Image, and set the text wrap to "in front of text" so you can move it over the signature line.
  • PDF: most readers (including the free viewer) have a "Fill & Sign" tool that lets you place a PNG image over the document.
  • Size: insert the signature at a realistic size relative to the line; a giant signature looks as off as a tiny one.

Image signature vs. digital signature: not the same

Worth clarifying so there are no surprises. Inserting an image of your signature into a document is perfect for informal agreements, internal documents, letters or forms that just ask you to "sign here". But it's not the same as a certified, legally valid electronic signature: processes that require an advanced electronic signature need an approved platform that verifies your identity. For everything else, an inserted PNG signature solves 90% of cases.

Your signature doesn't leave your device

A signature is sensitive data. In PhotoCut Studio the cutout happens entirely in your browser: the photo isn't uploaded to any server or stored. Even so, save the resulting PNG with the same care as your paper signature and don't share it needlessly.

We tested it: how much ink survives when the paper is removed

Image: firma-antes.jpg, 820 × 410 px, 13.7 KB (sample image from this site).

Tool: Cutout → Magic wand / Auto cutout.

What I did:

  1. I loaded the photo of a signature on paper.
  2. With “Magic wand” (default tolerance) I clicked once on the paper in the top-left corner and downloaded the PNG.
  3. In another tab I used “Auto cutout” on the same file and downloaded.
  4. I counted the dark pixels (luminance < 110 and opaque) in the original and in each PNG.

What I measured:

  • The original has 11416 ink (dark) pixels.
  • Magic wand: 0.11 s, 92.6% transparent, 92.4 KB PNG and 100.0% of the ink kept (11413 px).
  • Auto cutout: 20.01 s, 70.9% transparent, 116.0 KB and 100.0% of the ink kept (11416 px).

What went wrong or what limit I saw: The auto cutout took about 20 s and left a patch of light paper around the signature (almost 30% of the image stayed opaque), while the wand did it in a fraction of a second and clean. The wand leaves a light, semi-transparent halo around the stroke. I didn't paste the signature onto a PDF to see how that halo looks.

Tested by Gabriel Maglia on 2026-10-06 with PhotoCut (web) in Chrome 154.0.8037.98 · macOS 14.8.8 · PhotoCut version: commit bc142c7.
