Color accessibility: contrast and color blindness, explained simply

Updated: July 2026 · Reading time: 6 minutes · By Gabriel Maglia

A design can look beautiful and still be unreadable for part of your audience. Color accessibility comes down to two things that are easy to check: enough contrast so text can be read, and not relying on color alone to convey information. Both can be verified in a couple of minutes.

1. Contrast: so the text can be read

Contrast measures how different the text is from its background. It's expressed as a ratio, from 1:1 (invisible) to 21:1 (black on white). The WCAG accessibility guidelines set minimums:

  • Normal text: at least 4.5:1 (AA level), or 7:1 for AAA (stricter).
  • Large text (headings): 3:1 (AA) is enough.

The typical mistake is light-gray text on a white background: it looks "elegant" but many people can't read it. Before calling a combination good, measure the ratio with a contrast checker: it tells you if it passes AA/AAA for normal and large text.

2. Color blindness: don't rely on color alone

About 8% of men have some form of color blindness (and a smaller percentage of women). The most common cases confuse red and green. That breaks patterns we take for granted:

  • A chart that distinguishes categories only by red/green colors.
  • A form where an error is marked by "turning the field red".
  • A status light (green/yellow/red) with no text or icons.

The golden rule: color should never be the only signal. Always add something more —an icon, a word, a pattern, a different brightness— so the information is understood even if the color isn't perceived.

How to check your design in 2 steps

  1. Contrast: measure the text against its background with the checker; it should pass AA at minimum.
  2. Color blindness: simulate how your design looks with protanopia, deuteranopia, tritanopia and in grayscale. If something stops making sense, that information depended on color alone.

Quick best practices

  • Body text always with high contrast; reserve light grays for secondary details, not for reading.
  • States (success/error) with icon + text, not just color.
  • In charts, combine color with labels, patterns or shapes.
  • Try your palette in grayscale: if everything is distinguishable, you're on the right track.

All in your browser

Both the contrast checker and the color blindness simulator run locally: you upload an image or pick colors and everything is processed on your device, without uploading anything.

Frequently asked questions

Does accessibility affect SEO or just users?

It helps both: it improves the experience for everyone and is a valued best practice, but the main reason is that more people can use what you make.

We tested it: the contrast checker against my own WCAG arithmetic

Image: I didn't use any image: the tests are on loose colors.

Tool: Color Studio → Palette → Contrast (WCAG).

What I did:

  1. In Color Studio I entered five color pairs in “Text” and “Background” with the color picker (without loading any image).
  2. I noted the ratio and the four badges (AA normal, AA large, AAA normal, AAA large) that it shows.
  3. I calculated each ratio separately with the WCAG 2.x relative luminance formula and compared them.

What I measured:

  • #1f2937 on #d6f64b: 11.98:1 → ✓ AA · normal, ✓ AA · large, ✓ AAA · normal, ✓ AAA · large.
  • #777777 on #ffffff: 4.48:1 → ✕ AA · normal, ✓ AA · large, ✕ AAA · normal, ✕ AAA · large.
  • #767676 on #ffffff: 4.54:1 → ✓ AA · normal, ✓ AA · large, ✕ AAA · normal, ✓ AAA · large.
  • #2563eb on #ffffff: 5.17:1 → ✓ AA · normal, ✓ AA · large, ✕ AAA · normal, ✓ AAA · large.
  • #ffffff on #d6f64b: 1.22:1 → ✕ AA · normal, ✕ AA · large, ✕ AAA · normal, ✕ AAA · large.
  • My separate calculation matched the one the app shows, to two decimals, in 5 of 5 pairs.
  • I didn't measure times: it's an arithmetic calculation that updates when the color changes, and the latency of my measuring tool is greater than that of the calculation.

What went wrong or what limit I saw: The checker compares two flat colors: it doesn't measure text over photos or over gradients. In this test I didn't use the color-blindness simulation or measure times. The edge case is useful: #777777 on white (4.48:1) fails AA for normal text and #767676 (4.54:1) passes, by a very small margin.
