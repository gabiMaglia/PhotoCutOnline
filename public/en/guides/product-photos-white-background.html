Product photos for Amazon, Etsy and Shopify: sizes and white background

Updated: July 2026 · Reading time: 5 minutes · By Gabriel Maglia

Selling online starts with a good product photo, and almost every marketplace asks for the same thing: the product cut out on a white background, centered, with a little margin and in a square format. The catch is that each platform has its own size and rules. This guide brings them together and shows how to get the photo ready — removing the background and exporting at the right size — without installing anything and without uploading the image to any server.

Why the white background matters so much

  • Amazon requires it on the main photo: if the background isn't pure white, they can reject or suppress the listing.
  • Catalog consistency: with every photo on the same white, your store looks professional and the products "float".
  • Conversion: with no background distractions, attention goes to the product. It's the e-commerce standard for a reason.

How to get the photo ready, step by step

  1. Open the product photo in PhotoCut Studio (drag the file in or paste with Ctrl/Cmd+V).
  2. Remove the background: try the AI Cutout; if the product has complex edges, adjust with the keep/remove brushes.
  3. In Export, choose the Color background mode and set white (#ffffff).
  4. Pick your platform's size preset (Amazon 2000×2000, Etsy 2700×2025, Shopify 2048×2048, Instagram 1080×1080): the preset crops to the product, centers it and adds margin automatically.
  5. Download. Repeat the same cutout for each platform by changing only the preset.

Tricks to make it look pro

  • Breathing margin: leave 5–10% of air around the product; up against the edges it looks cramped. The presets already do this.
  • Subtle shadow: a soft shadow under the product adds volume and avoids the "cut with scissors" look. PhotoCut adds it as an optional finish.
  • Work in high resolution: cut out on the largest photo you have; marketplaces reward zoom.
  • Check the edges against white before exporting (the white-background preview reveals any halo).

Mistakes that reject or penalize a listing

  • "Almost" white background. A grayish or yellow-tinted white from the original lighting won't pass Amazon's check. Force the exact #ffffff color in the export mode; don't trust "it looks white" by eye.
  • Shadow or reflection from the original product sneaking in. If the automatic cutout didn't catch the edge well, a dark remnant can stay at the base of the product and show against white.
  • Watermark, text or overlaid logo on the main image: most marketplaces explicitly forbid it on the MAIN photo.
  • Too tight a crop. With no breathing margin, the product looks squeezed against the frame and the listing thumbnails cut it off.
  • Insufficient resolution. Uploading an 800×800 photo as if it were 2000×2000 leaves it blurry on zoom, which lowers buyer confidence.

Privacy: your photos aren't uploaded

Unlike cloud cutout services, in PhotoCut all processing happens in your browser. Your product photos — often of unreleased items — never leave your device.

Frequently asked questions

Does it work for photos taken with a phone at home?

Yes, as long as the product is well lit and in focus. Automatic cutout depends on the contrast between the product and what's behind it; a plain table under natural light usually gives better results than a cluttered background.

We tested it: a synthetic mug at 2000 × 2000 px on white

Image: producto-sintetico.jpg, 1200 × 1200 px, 29.1 KB (synthetic image I drew with Pillow).

Tool: Cutout → AI cutout, “Amazon 2000×2000” preset, Export → Color #ffffff.

What I did:

  1. I loaded an image I drew myself (a blue mug with a shadow on a gradient gray) and applied “AI cutout”.
  2. I chose the “Amazon 2000×2000” preset.
  3. With “Transparent” + PNG I downloaded to see what the preset did with the transparency.
  4. I chose “Color” mode #ffffff, JPEG format and downloaded; I measured the box of the pixels that aren't almost white.

What I measured:

  • AI cutout: 4.2 s on 1200 × 1200 px.
  • The preset produced a 2000 × 2000 px canvas. The product (pixels that aren't almost white) takes up 89.3% of the canvas width and 74.6% of its height.
  • JPEG on white: 90 KB, 40.3% of pixels exactly #ffffff, non-white corners 0.0%. The PNG requested as “Transparent” with the preset weighed 586 KB and came out with 0.0% transparent pixels.

What went wrong or what limit I saw: The image is a mug I drew with flat colors: it's the easiest case for the AI, and I didn't test a shiny product or one with hard shadows, where it can fail. With the Amazon preset the “Transparent” mode came out opaque, because the preset brings its own white background: there's no way to ask for a transparent PNG at 2000 × 2000 with that preset.
