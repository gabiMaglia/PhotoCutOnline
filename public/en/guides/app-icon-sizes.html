App icon sizes in 2026: iOS, Android, macOS, Windows and Web

Updated: July 2026 · Reading time: 6 minutes · By Gabriel Maglia

Each platform asks for icons its own way: Apple wants an appiconset with light/dark/tinted variants, Android moved to adaptive icons with a monochrome layer for Material You themes, and the web has its own little zoo of favicons. This guide gathers every current size in 2026 in one place.

iOS / iPadOS (Xcode 16+)

Apple documents a layer size of 1024 × 1024 px for iOS and iPadOS, and Xcode can generate all the variations from a single image of that size. The current design guideline lists the default, dark, “clear” and “tinted” appearances (the last two in light and dark versions) and the system generates the ones you don't supply. The three that get designed the most, at the same size:

VariantSizeNotes
Standard (any)1024 × 1024No transparency or rounded corners: the system applies the mask.
Dark1024 × 1024Transparent or dark background; the subject must work over the system's dark background.
Tinted1024 × 1024Grayscale; iOS tints it with the user's accent color.

Official sources (verified on 2026-10-06): Apple, Human Interface Guidelines, “App icons” (1024×1024 px and appearances) and Apple, “Configuring your app icon using an asset catalog” (the variations can be generated from a single 1024×1024 px image). Those pages don't detail the table's notes (no alpha in the standard one, dark background in Dark, grayscale in Tinted): they are no official source, common value, just like the legacy sizes below.

For projects still supporting older versions, the legacy sizes remain: 20, 29, 40, 60, 76 and 83.5 pt at @1x/@2x/@3x per device (40, 58, 60, 80, 87, 120, 152, 167, 180 px…).

Android (API 33+ / Android 13)

Official sources (verified on 2026-10-06), all from Android Developers: “Support different pixel densities” (ratio 3:4:6:8:12:16 over 48×48 px at mdpi: 48, 72, 96, 144 and 192 px), “Adaptive icons” (108×108 dp layers; the logo must not exceed 66×66 dp, which at xxxhdpi are 432 and 264 px) and “Google Play icon design specifications” (512×512 px, 32-bit PNG, sRGB, up to 1024 KB).

macOS

An AppIcon.iconset with 10 files that's then compiled to .icns with iconutil. Apple documents 1024 × 1024 px as the layer size for macOS (same source as iOS, above) and asks you to supply one image per variation in Xcode; the 10-file list below is the one iconutil expects and it doesn't appear on those pages (no official source, common value): 

16, 16@2x, 32, 32@2x, 128, 128@2x, 256, 256@2x, 512, 512@2x
(that is: 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512 and 1024 px)

Windows

A single multi-resolution app.ico with 7 embedded sizes: 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 128 and 256 px. Installers and the taskbar pick the one they need. Official source: Microsoft Learn, “Construct your Windows App's Icon” (verified on 2026-10-06), which asks for at least 16, 24, 32, 48 and 256 px. The 64 and 128 px ones are added by Icon Studio and aren't in that minimum.

Official sources for the web table (verified on 2026-10-06): Google Search Central, “Define Website Favicon for Search Results” (square favicon, minimum 8×8 px and recommended larger than 48×48 px); Apple, “Configuring Web Applications” (180×180 px apple-touch-icon, among others); web.dev, “Add a web app manifest” (192×192 and 512×512 px icons) and web.dev, “Maskable icons” (safe zone: a central circle with a radius equal to 40 % of the width). The 32 px favicon is no official source, common value.

Design rules that save headaches

  • Work from a transparent PNG of 1024 px or more and generate everything downward.
  • Breathing margin: 8–12% around the art; on adaptive/maskable, remember the system crops the edges.
  • Check at small sizes: an icon that looks good at 256 px can be unreadable at 16. If it isn't recognizable at 32 px, simplify.
  • No small text: it disappears at taskbar sizes.

Common mistakes preparing the source art

  • Starting from a small size. Scaling a 256 px icon up to 1024 leaves it blurry. Always design or export from the largest file available.
  • Leaving transparency in the standard iOS icon. The standard iOS icon carries no transparency: if your PNG has it, fill it with a background before handing it in, because what happens to unfilled alpha depends on the build tool.
  • Ignoring the adaptive/maskable safe zone. Content outside the central circle (264 px over 432 on Android, central 80% on maskable) gets cropped by whatever shape each launcher uses — square, circle, squircle or teardrop.
  • One design for dark and tinted. The iOS 18 tinted variant shows in grayscale with the user's accent color: an icon with many different colors loses all contrast when it goes monochrome if it wasn't designed with that in mind.
  • Naming the iconset/appiconset files wrong. Xcode and iconutil expect exact names (icon_16x16@2x.png, etc.); a different name and the build fails or uses the default icon.

Where to start if I only have one logo?

If your starting point is a logo with a transparent background and none of these rules in mind, the recommended order is: 1) remove the background and keep only the symbol (not the full wordmark with text, which becomes unreadable at 16 px); 2) center it on a square 1024×1024 canvas with 10–15% margin; 3) generate the full per-platform set from there. That first clean cutout is the step that saves the most time later.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between Android's adaptive and maskable icon?

They're equivalent concepts in different ecosystems: the adaptive icon is Android's native format (foreground + background, cropped by the system into different shapes), and the maskable icon is its PWA equivalent declared in the manifest. Both require the important content to fit in the central safe zone.
