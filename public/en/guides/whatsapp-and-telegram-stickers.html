WhatsApp and Telegram stickers: requirements, comparison table and how to make them free

Updated: October 2026 · Reading time: 6 minutes · By Gabriel Maglia

A sticker that looks like a real sticker —and not like a rectangular photo pasted into the chat— depends on two things: cutting out the background well and meeting the exact format of the app where you'll use it. WhatsApp and Telegram ask for similar but not identical things, so here's a single guide: the cutout workflow is shared, and what changes is the export and how you upload it. The whole cutout happens in your browser, without uploading the photo to any server.

WhatsApp and Telegram, side by side

The shared workflow: from photo to sticker

  1. Open your photo in PhotoCut Studio (drag it in or paste with Ctrl/Cmd+V).
  2. Remove the background with the AI Cutout and refine the edges with the brushes if needed.
  3. Turn on the outline (optional but recommended) for the classic cut-out sticker look.
  4. Frame the subject with an even margin: it shouldn't touch the edges of the frame.
  5. Export for your destination, as explained next.

For WhatsApp: 512×512 WebP under 100 KB

In Export choose Transparent and the WEBP format, download, and take the result to 512 px in the File tab (WebP, at whatever quality it takes to stay under 100 KB). WhatsApp asks for exactly 512×512 px: the File panel only scales, so frame it square first. WebP compresses better than PNG while keeping transparency, which is why meeting the weight limit is much easier than with a PNG of the same size. Besides the sticker, the pack asks for:

  • Tray (cover) icon: 96 × 96 px, WebP, under 50 KB.
  • Count: minimum 3 and maximum 30 stickers per pack.
  • A separate app: WhatsApp doesn't let you add a loose sticker from the gallery; you need a pack app that registers it. Download the .webp and use it there.

If the file doesn't meet these specs, pack apps reject it or resize it badly, leaving blurry or cropped edges.

For Telegram: 512 px transparent PNG and the @Stickers bot

In Export choose Transparent mode and PNG (JPG doesn't work: it fills the background), download and, in the File tab, set the longest side to 512 px. To upload it, Telegram doesn't use third-party apps but its own bot:

  1. Search for and open the chat with @Stickers.
  2. Use the command to create a new pack and name it.
  3. Send your PNG with transparency and assign it an emoji.
  4. Repeat for each sticker; when you finish, the bot gives you a link to use and share the pack.

Why the outline makes the difference

A sticker with no border at all can "disappear" if the chat background is a similar color to the cut-out subject. A thin white (or colored) outline around the silhouette visually separates the sticker from the chat background, gives it a more polished finish and hides small imperfections left on the edge after the cutout. If your sticker looks "flat" on the bubble, try giving it an outline or a small margin.

Common mistakes when making stickers

  • Exporting the whole photo rectangle. Without removing the background it looks like a pasted photo, not a sticker: the cutout isn't optional.
  • Going over the weight on WhatsApp. A badly compressed WebP can exceed 100 KB and the sticker app rejects it without saying why.
  • Using PNG for WhatsApp. A PNG of the same size weighs quite a bit more than an equivalent WebP and makes the limit harder to meet; PNG is for Telegram.
  • Subject touching the frame edge. Without a margin it looks cramped and the outline gets cut at the canvas edge.
  • Tiny text. Stickers are shown in the chat at a reduced size; fine details that read well in the large preview can get lost in real use.

From a single sticker to a full pack

WhatsApp requires at least three stickers per pack, so it pays to prepare several variants at once: different expressions of a pet, different poses or different texts over the same cut-out base. Repeat the same cutout and export flow for each, with the same outline style, so the pack looks coherent. On Telegram the bot builds the pack as you send the PNGs. And since the cutout is already done, you can export the same subject in both versions: WebP for WhatsApp and PNG for Telegram.

We tested it: the sticker's weight against WhatsApp's 100 KB limit

Image: quitar-fondo-antes.jpg, 720 × 900 px, 78.4 KB (public-domain portrait).

Tool: Cutout → AI cutout + “Sticker (outline)”, and File → WebP.

What I did:

  1. I loaded the portrait and applied “AI cutout”; I downloaded a transparent PNG with no outline as a reference.
  2. I turned on “Sticker (outline)” with the default width (14 px) and downloaded as PNG and as WebP.
  3. I opened the WebP in the “File” tab, took it to 512 px tall at quality 80 and downloaded.

What I measured:

  • Without outline, PNG: 720 × 900 px, 535 KB. With a 14 px white outline: PNG 720 × 900 px, 529 KB, and WebP 51 KB with 44.1% transparent pixels.
  • That WebP, taken in File to 410 × 512 px (quality 80): 15.0 KB and 43.5% transparent. WhatsApp's limit is 100 KB.
  • The editor let me set the height to 512 and the width adjusted itself to 410 px (the photo is vertical). A 512 × 512 square, which is what WhatsApp asks for, would have required stretching the image or cropping it first.

What went wrong or what limit I saw: The Stickers tab (with the “WhatsApp · WebP 512” destination) is disabled in this version of the site, so I couldn't use it: with what's there, the sticker comes out at 720 × 900 px and has to be taken to 512 tall in File. An exact 512 × 512, which is what WhatsApp asks for, I didn't find how to get from a vertical photo without stretching it: File only scales and I didn't try framing beforehand with “Crop area”. I didn't build the pack or test it inside WhatsApp or Telegram.
