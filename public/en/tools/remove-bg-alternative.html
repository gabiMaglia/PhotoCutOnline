The part almost nobody checks: where your photo goes

Any service that processes in the cloud must, by design, receive your file. For a holiday snapshot that hardly matters. For a client's document, an ID, an invoice or material under NDA, it does: you are handing that file to a third party.

Here the AI model downloads once and runs in your browser. The image does not travel: no upload, no copy on a server, no retention policy to read. That is a structural difference, not a promise.

Where remove.bg is still the better pick

For this comparison to be worth anything, it has to say so:

  • Mature API: if you need background removal inside a backend, or thousands of images processed automatically, their API is built for that. PhotoCut is a tool for people, not a service to integrate.
  • Edge cases: a large cloud model can handle very fine hair or busy backgrounds better than a lightweight one running on your device.
  • Automated volume: our batch mode is comfortable for dozens of photos, not for a 50,000-item catalog with no human in the loop.

If that is your case, remove.bg is a solid choice. If you just want to cut out your own photos, at full resolution, without paying per image and without handing them over, PhotoCut fits better.

How to cut out here

FAQ

Why does remove.bg only give me a low-resolution download?

Its free plan returns previews of up to 0.25 MP; full resolution consumes paid credits. That is the business model, not a bug.

Does PhotoCut cap resolution or use credits?

No: you export at your original's resolution, with no credits and no usage cap. Processing on your device means no per-image cost to ration.

Is my photo uploaded to a server?

No. The model runs in your browser and the image never leaves your machine.

Is the quality comparable?

On clear subjects, very close. On hard cases the cloud may win — which is why we add brushes to refine by hand.
