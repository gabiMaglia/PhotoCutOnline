When black is the best choice

  • Premium products & tech: a gadget, a watch or a bottle on black looks expensive and sophisticated.
  • Jewelry: metal and stones sparkle against black in a way they don't against white.
  • Food: a dish on a dark background looks dramatic and appetizing.
  • Portraits: all attention goes to the face and expression.

The key detail: don't let the subject merge

The main challenge of a black background is the opposite of white: instead of exposing leftovers, it swallows dark edges. If the subject has black hair, dark clothing or shadowed areas, those edges can vanish into pure black. Two fixes:

  • Use a very dark grey (#0a0a0a or #111111) instead of pure black: it keeps the effect but lets the outline breathe.
  • Start from a photo with some rim light on the subject's edge, which naturally separates it from the background.

Step by step

  1. Open the photo in the editor.
  2. Use the AI Cutout and refine the edges (watch the subject's dark areas).
  3. In Export, pick Color mode and set #000000 (or #0a0a0a).
  4. Export as JPG or PNG.

Frequently asked questions

Pure black or a very dark grey?

Pure black (#000000) gives maximum drama; a very dark grey (#0a0a0a) keeps the effect and helps separate the outline if the subject is also dark.

Why does my subject disappear into the black?

Its dark edges have low contrast against the background. Check the outline zoomed in and try a slightly lighter-than-black background.

Is my photo uploaded to a server?

No. Everything happens in your browser; the photo is never uploaded or stored.
